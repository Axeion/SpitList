FH SERIES

1500, rest-of-world

Continues the numbering Triumph started with the MkIV. Used on every pre-VIN 1500 outside North America, beginning in 1975.

FM SERIES

1500, North America

A new sequence opened in 1973 to bring the 1500 to the US and Canada while the rest of the world was still getting the MkIV.

The Car

A Herald underneath, a Michelotti body on top.

Origin, 1962

Giovanni Michelotti drew the Spitfire around the Triumph Herald's frame, drivetrain, and suspension. It arrived in October 1962 as Triumph's answer to the small British sports car class, going head to head with the MG Midget and its rivals.

What set it apart was the Herald-derived independent suspension and roll-up windows — comfort and handling usually reserved for cars costing a good deal more.

"Same silhouette, three series, one long-running argument about cornering stability."

Engines, across four decades

The Spitfire began with a 1,147cc four borrowed from the Herald line. The Mk3 grew it to 1,296cc with a redesigned four-port head. After the MkIV, the block was stroked out to 1,493cc — though later emissions equipment, especially on US-market cars, quietly ate into the gains.

Michelotti himself updated the design again for the 1971 MkIV, keeping the silhouette but modernizing the details and reworking the rear suspension for better cornering manners.

How It's Built

Contributed by clubs. Corrected by owners.

This isn't a factory record — it's decades of club newsletters, owner submissions, and monthly updates, with all the mess that implies.

01

Data gets stale

Entries come from clubs around the world and can lag behind reality — cars change hands, get parted out, or vanish, long before a club roster catches up. Spot an error and there's a form to fix it.

02

Model year is judgment

Where manufacturing date and first registration disagree, registration wins — dealers often re-badged unsold cars to the next model year, so the factory sequence is treated as the tiebreaker.

03

Modified cars still count

V6 and V8 swaps, the GT6-engined "Spit 6" conversion — all included, as long as the car still carries its original commission number. Lose the plate and it drops off the list.

Own one? Get it on the books.
