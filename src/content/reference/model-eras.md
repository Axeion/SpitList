The registry is organised by production series, because that is how Triumph built
the car and how the numbering works. Live counts for each era are on the
[registry](/registry) — this page is about what actually changed.

## Mk1 — Spitfire 4, 1962

Giovanni Michelotti's body over the Triumph Herald's frame, drivetrain and
independent rear suspension, powered by the Herald's **1,147cc** four. Roll-up
windows in a class that mostly did without them, and a bonnet that hinges forward
as one piece.

## Mk2, 1965

Same silhouette and the same 1,147cc engine, with a warmer camshaft and a tidier
interior. The commission-number sequence continues straight on from the Mk1
rather than restarting, so the number alone will not separate a late Mk1 from an
early Mk2.

## Mk3, 1967

The engine goes out to **1,296cc**, and the front bumper is raised — the quickest
visual identification of a Mk3 from the front, and a direct answer to US
regulations.

## MkIV, 1971

Michelotti back for a second pass: the tail is squared off, the interior
substantially reworked, and — the change that mattered most — the rear suspension
is redesigned to settle the swing-axle cornering behaviour the earlier cars were
criticised for.

## 1500, 1973

The block is stroked to **1,493cc**. North America gets the 1500 first, in 1973,
under its own `FM` numbering, while the rest of the world is still buying MkIVs;
rest-of-world 1500s continue the MkIV's `FH` sequence from 1975. Emissions
equipment on US-market cars ate most of the power the extra capacity provided.

From October 1979 the last cars carry [VINs](/reference/vin) rather than
commission numbers. Production ended in 1981.
