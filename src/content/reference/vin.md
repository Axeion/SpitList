In **October 1979**, partway through the model year, Triumph replaced commission
numbers with VINs under the international standard. Spitfires built from that
point carry a VIN and no commission number.

This is why the registry keeps the VIN cars as their own series rather than
continuing the `FH` sequence: the numbering restarts under a different scheme, and
model-year labels do not track cleanly against it.

## Anatomy of the number

The late-Spitfire VIN runs **five letters, one digit, two letters, then six
digits**:

```
TFADW 5 AT 009898
│     │ │  │
│     │ │  └── sequential build number
│     │ └───── model / market code
│     └─────── steering and overdrive
└───────────── model prefix
```

`TFADW5AT009898` is the last Spitfire built.

### The steering and overdrive digit

The single digit after the five-letter prefix is the most useful character to
know, because it describes the car rather than the sequence:

| Digit | Steering | Overdrive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1` | Right-hand drive | No |
| `2` | Left-hand drive | No |
| `5` | Right-hand drive | Yes |
| `6` | Left-hand drive | Yes |

The [decoder](/reference/decoder) reads this digit for you.

## What is not decoded here

The five-letter prefix varies across late cars — `TFADW` and `TFVDW` both appear —
and the two letters before the sequence carry model and market information. This
registry has not sourced a position-by-position table against a primary record, so
it does not publish one. Guessing at the remaining characters would produce
confident wrong answers, which is worse than an honest gap.

For any individual car, a
[British Motor Heritage certificate](/reference/heritage-certificates) is the
authoritative answer.
