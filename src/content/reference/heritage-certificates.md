A **Heritage Certificate** is a certified copy of your car's entry in the original
factory ledger — the specification it actually left the production line with.

The records are held by the **British Motor Industry Heritage Trust**, whose
archive lives at the British Motor Museum in Gaydon, Warwickshire. Their Triumph
holdings cover all postwar models from **1946 to 1981**, which is the entire
Spitfire run. The Triumph ledgers are among the archives that have been digitised,
so requests are not usually held up by the fragile-microfilm problem that affects
some other marques.

Order one here: [britishmotormuseum.co.uk/archive/heritage-certificates](https://www.britishmotormuseum.co.uk/archive/heritage-certificates)

## Why it matters for the registry

Almost everything this registry cannot tell you about an individual car, a
Heritage Certificate can:

- the original colour and trim, rather than the colour it wears now
- the build date, against which a registration date can be judged
- the engine and gearbox numbers as fitted at the factory
- where the car was originally despatched

If you order one, the details on it are exactly what makes a good submission —
and a good correction. Mention the certificate in the note to moderators and your
entry effectively arrives pre-verified.

## Beyond the certificate

Certificates are also commonly needed when applying for tax exemption or an
age-related registration mark, or when importing or exporting a car. That is
outside what this registry does, but it is usually why people order one in the
first place.
